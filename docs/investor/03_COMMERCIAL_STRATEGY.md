# AICIS — Commercial Strategy

## Commercial thesis

AICIS should not enter the market as a general-purpose “civilization intelligence” platform. The commercial entry point is a focused workflow where fragmented signals, slow reconciliation and weak provenance create measurable operational cost or risk.

**Initial wedge:** evidence-governed resilience and continuity intelligence for public institutions and critical-infrastructure operators.

The long-term multi-domain vision remains an expansion path, not the first sales message.

## Initial customer profile

### Primary organizations

- municipalities and regional public bodies;
- public utilities;
- transport and mobility operators;
- telecom and digital-infrastructure operators;
- energy and facilities operators;
- resilience / continuity teams in larger institutions.

### Primary users

- resilience and continuity analysts;
- incident / operations coordinators;
- risk and intelligence teams;
- authorized decision-makers who review consequential recommendations.

### Economic / operational buyer

The buyer is the function accountable for continuity, incident readiness, risk intelligence or operational resilience. The exact budget owner must be discovered through interviews and procurement conversations rather than assumed.

## Problem statement

The commercial problem to validate is not “organizations need more AI.” It is:

> Teams must combine fragmented, fast-changing evidence before they can decide what matters, what is uncertain and what action requires review.

AICIS competes first against the current workflow: manual monitoring, disconnected dashboards, spreadsheets, email / chat escalation and analyst reconciliation.

## Value hypotheses to test

AICIS must earn commercial value through measurable outcomes such as:

- lower time-to-triage;
- fewer hours spent reconciling duplicate or conflicting sources;
- earlier identification of relevant events;
- better provenance coverage;
- more consistent escalation and review;
- improved auditability of how a decision was reached.

These are hypotheses until measured.

## Go-to-market sequence

### Phase 1 — discovery

Conduct structured interviews with target organizations. Record:

- current workflow;
- frequency and cost of the problem;
- existing tools and contracts;
- data-access constraints;
- procurement path;
- security / privacy requirements;
- willingness to run a bounded pilot;
- willingness to pay.

### Phase 2 — design partner

Secure one partner around one workflow. Use the pilot blueprint in `02_PILOT_BLUEPRINT.md`.

### Phase 3 — paid validation

Prefer a paid pilot where procurement permits. If an unpaid research/design pilot is necessary, require a defined evaluation protocol and a documented conversion decision.

### Phase 4 — repeatability

Do not claim product-market fit after one success. The next proof point is a second independent customer with materially similar needs and a repeatable deployment pattern.

### Phase 5 — expansion

Expand through:

- additional teams;
- additional data sources;
- additional event / risk workflows;
- integrations;
- broader geographic or operational coverage.

## Revenue-model hypothesis

The model to validate is:

1. **design-partner / implementation fee** where appropriate;
2. **annual enterprise software subscription**;
3. optional charges for high-cost integrations, data processing or specialized deployment requirements.

Exact prices, margins and contract values must be learned from buyer evidence. They should not be presented to investors as validated before real procurement conversations.

## Competitive framing

AICIS should not claim “no competitors.” The relevant alternatives include:

- manual analyst workflows;
- monitoring and alerting platforms;
- geospatial / operational dashboards;
- risk-intelligence platforms;
- data-integration products;
- general-purpose LLM / agent tooling;
- internal data / analytics teams.

The differentiated thesis is the combination of heterogeneous-signal fusion, provenance, uncertainty-aware analysis, human review and reusable evaluation history inside one governed workflow.

## Moat-building plan

Defensibility should compound through:

- customer-specific integrations that become reusable connectors;
- normalized event / entity representations;
- evidence lineage and evaluation data;
- accumulated human review and labelled outcomes;
- calibrated domain workflows;
- security, governance and audit controls;
- deployment knowledge in regulated / public-institution contexts.

A model provider can be replaced. The governed workflow, data history and institutional integration should become harder to replace.

## Evidence gates for investor claims

| Claim | Minimum evidence before saying it |
|---|---|
| “Customers” | signed commercial relationship or paid contract |
| “Pilot” | signed pilot / design-partner scope |
| “Revenue” | invoiced / received revenue records |
| “Traction” | verifiable customer, usage, procurement or revenue evidence |
| “Validated forecasting” | reproducible evaluation on defined datasets / contexts |
| “Improves decisions” | controlled or baseline-comparative measured outcome |
| “Production ready” | defined production gates passed in the target deployment context |
| “Product-market fit” | repeatable demand, retention / expansion and sustainable commercial evidence |

## Current commercial status

AICIS is currently **pre-commercial validation**. The appropriate external description is:

> A technically advanced pre-seed project preparing for focused institutional pilots and commercial validation.
